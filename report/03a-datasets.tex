\paragraph{D1 make\_moons.} % Lam. lam/src/sc4020/data.py (load_make_moons)
Two interleaving half-circles from scikit-learn's \texttt{make\_moons}~\cite{pedregosa2011}: 1{,}000 points,
Gaussian noise 0.07, seed 42, then standardised. The two classes are dense, equal in size and clearly
separated by a gap, but neither is convex: each moon wraps around the end of the other, so the
straight boundary a centroid method draws must cut both. With the true labels known and no other
difficulty present, D1 isolates cluster shape as the single property under test.

\paragraph{D5 Fashion-MNIST.} % Lam. lam/src/sc4020/data.py (load_fashion_mnist), lam/notebooks/03_fashion_mnist.ipynb
Fashion-MNIST~\cite{xiao2017} holds 70{,}000 $28\times28$ greyscale images of 10 clothing classes.
We pool the train and test files, scale pixels to $[0,1]$, draw a class-stratified sample of 3{,}000
images (300 per class, seed 42), and project the 784 pixels onto 50 principal components, which keep
86.5\% of the variance. The sample keeps Spectral, DBSCAN and HDBSCAN within reach on a laptop
(Section~\ref{sec:runtime}) while keeping the classes balanced. D5 is hard because the space is high
dimensional, so distances concentrate~\cite{beyer1999}, and because several classes (shirt, T-shirt,
pullover, coat) overlap heavily with no density gap between them.
\paragraph{D6 Wine.} % Lam: lam/src/sc4020/data.py (load_wine_data), lam/results/wine_results.csv
The UCI Wine data~\cite{wine1991}: 178 wines from three cultivars, described by 13 chemical
measurements, all standardised before clustering. The cultivars form compact, roughly Gaussian groups,
and K-Means++ with $k=3$ reaches ARI 0.897. D6 is hard for the density methods instead: with only 178
points in 13 dimensions there are too few neighbours for a stable density estimate, and the score that
looks best (silhouette) does not pick the partition that matches the labels (Section~\ref{sec:silhouette}).

\paragraph{D1 make\_moons.}
Two interleaving half-circles from scikit-learn's \texttt{make\_moons}~\cite{pedregosa2011}: 1{,}000 points,
Gaussian noise 0.07, seed 42, then standardised. The two classes are dense, equal in size and clearly
separated by a gap, but neither is convex: each moon wraps around the end of the other, so the
straight boundary a centroid method draws must cut both. With the true labels known and no other
difficulty present, D1 isolates cluster shape as the single property under test.
\paragraph{D2 Earthquakes.}
% Source: experiments/07_usgs_earthquakes_2023_eda.ipynb.
Every event of magnitude 4.5 or more in 2023 from the USGS ComCat catalogue~\cite{usgs2017}: 7{,}651 events, of which
8 volcanic eruptions near Saipan are removed, leaving 7{,}643 earthquakes. We cluster the epicentre only.
Depth is left out because 44.2\% of events carry the default depth of 10\,km. Each method meets a
different difficulty. Earthquakes lie along narrow belts thousands of kilometres long, which breaks the
compact-cluster assumption of K-means. Density varies widely: the distance to the nearest earthquake
runs from 1.2\,km (5th percentile) to 100.5\,km (95th), so no single DBSCAN radius suits every belt.
And 17.2\% of earthquakes lie within $10^\circ$ of the $180^\circ$ meridian, where flat coordinates
cut the map. There are no labels.
\paragraph{D3 Uber NYC.}
% Source: experiments/13_multi_source_hotspot_clustering.ipynb; results/hotspot/case_stats.csv.
Uber pickups in New York City in April 2014, from the trip records the city's Taxi and Limousine
Commission released to FiveThirtyEight~\cite{fivethirtyeight2014}: 564{,}516 pickups, each a latitude
and longitude. We keep the 558{,}019 inside a box around the city (latitude 40.57 to 40.92, longitude
$-74.15$ to $-73.70$), which drops GPS errors, and sample 15{,}000 at random (seed 42). The coordinates
are projected to kilometres about the mean latitude, so $\varepsilon$ is a distance on the ground.
Pickups crowd into lower and midtown Manhattan (71\% of the sample), with smaller dense spots at the two
airports and sparse demand in the outer boroughs. The distance to the 4th nearest pickup runs from
0.008\,km to 0.256\,km (5th to 95th percentile), but the dense and sparse areas meet with no clear gap
between them. There are no labels.
\paragraph{D4 Ecoli.}
% Source: results/hotspot/case_stats.csv (class sizes).
The UCI Ecoli data~\cite{horton1996}: 336 proteins of \emph{E.~coli}, each described by seven scores
computed from its amino-acid sequence and labelled with one of eight sites in the cell where the protein
is found. Features are standardised. The classes are very unequal: the five largest hold 143, 77, 52, 35
and 20 proteins, the three smallest 5, 2 and 2. D4 is hard for density methods in two ways. Seven
dimensions already spread the points so that a density threshold separates less well, and the rare
classes are too small to form a dense region of their own, so a density method tends to call them noise.
\paragraph{D5 Fashion-MNIST.}
Fashion-MNIST~\cite{xiao2017} holds 70{,}000 $28\times28$ greyscale images of 10 clothing classes.
We pool the train and test files, scale pixels to $[0,1]$, draw a class-stratified sample of 3{,}000
images (300 per class, seed 42), and project the 784 pixels onto 50 principal components, which keep
86.5\% of the variance. The sample keeps Spectral, DBSCAN and HDBSCAN within reach on a laptop
(Section~\ref{sec:runtime}) while keeping the classes balanced. D5 is hard because the space is high
dimensional, so distances concentrate~\cite{beyer1999}, and because several classes (shirt, T-shirt,
pullover, coat) overlap heavily with no density gap between them.
\paragraph{D6 Wine.}
% Source: results/comparison/report_d6_d7.csv (experiments/01_scikit_learn_wine_clustering.ipynb).
The UCI Wine data~\cite{aeberhard1991}: 178 wines grown in one Italian region, each described by 13
chemical measurements such as alcohol, malic acid, flavanoids and proline. The cultivar (3 classes of
59, 71 and 48 wines) is held out as the label. Features are standardised, because proline runs from
278 to 1{,}680 while hue stays below 2. The classes are compact, so K-means recovers them well (ARI
0.897). The dataset is in the study for the opposite reason. No sparse gap separates the cultivars, so
the density methods return 2 clusters with a quarter to two fifths of the wines as noise, and the
silhouette still prefers them (Section~\ref{sec:silhouette}).
\paragraph{D7 HDB resale.}
% Source: experiments/10_data_gov_hdb_resale_eda.ipynb; floor-area baseline from
% experiments/11_data_gov_hdb_resale_clustering.ipynb.
Every resale of a public housing flat in Singapore from January 2017, from data.gov.sg~\cite{hdb2026}. The
8{,}245 most recent sales (3.4\%) have no price index value yet and are dropped, leaving 234{,}292. Methods
are compared on a random sample of 20{,}000 (seed 0); all 234{,}292 are used for runtime. There are four
features: floor area, remaining lease, storey, and price adjusted to 2026 levels with the HDB resale
price index. Flat type (7 classes) is held out as a label, but it is nearly a function of size: K-means
on floor area alone reaches ARI 0.632 against it. The data are hard for density methods. There are no
gaps between groups of flats, and storeys are recorded in 3-storey bands, so 77.7\% of sales share their
floor area, lease and storey with at least one other sale.

\subsection{Preprocessing}\label{sec:prep}
Three rules apply wherever a dataset has the property they address.

\paragraph{Coordinates on the sphere.}
% Source: experiments/07_usgs_earthquakes_2023_eda.ipynb.
Latitude and longitude treated as flat numbers put two earthquakes 6.07\,km apart at $359.94^\circ$ of
longitude from each other, because they lie on either side of the $180^\circ$ meridian. On D2, density
methods therefore use the haversine (great-circle) distance on coordinates in radians, while K-means, GMM
and spectral clustering work on each epicentre as a 3D point on the unit sphere. The straight-line
distance between two such points grows with their great-circle distance, so the clustering follows
surface distance. D3 covers half a degree of one city, where a projection to kilometres is
accurate.

\paragraph{Standardised features.}
% Source: experiments/10_data_gov_hdb_resale_eda.ipynb.
Features in their raw units are not comparable. On D7, price (in dollars) accounts for 100.0\% of the
squared distance between sales, so the other three features are ignored. Every feature is scaled to mean
0 and standard deviation 1.

\paragraph{Values recorded in steps.}
% Source: src/sc4020/datasets/preprocess_dataset.py, jitter_ties.
When a feature is recorded in steps, many points share exact values, and density methods read each shared
value as a dense spot. Before running a density method on D7, each stepped value is moved by a uniform
random amount of up to half its step: $\pm1.5$ storeys, $\pm0.5$\,m$^2$ of floor area and
$\pm\frac{1}{24}$ year of lease. Price is continuous and is left unchanged.

\subsection{Success and failure cases}\label{sec:cases}

% Rubric: "illustrate and discuss the successful cases and failure cases", "good visualizations".

\subsubsection{Successes}
\paragraph{HDBSCAN on D2: belts of varying density.} Shown in Figure~\ref{fig:density}. HDBSCAN keeps
the sparse belts of the Americas (62\% of earthquakes clustered, against 20\% for DBSCAN) as long
clusters that follow the plate boundary. It succeeds because its density threshold adapts to each
cluster, the property D2 needs most.
\paragraph{Spectral clustering on D1.} % results/comparison/report_d1.csv
Spectral clustering follows the curve of each moon and matches the labels exactly (ARI 1.000;
Figure~\ref{fig:lam-d1}). It succeeds because each moon is a chain of close neighbours, and the gap
between the moons is wider than any link in the chain, so the neighbour graph falls apart into the two
moons. K-Means++ and GMM fail on the same data because they assume convex clusters.

\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{lam_d1_moons_grid}
% Six of the nine panels of results/datasets/figures/make_moons_clusters.png.
\caption{D1 make\_moons at the settings chosen without labels. K-Means++ and GMM cut both moons with a
straight boundary (ARI 0.48, 0.50); spectral clustering follows the curves (ARI 1.00). DBSCAN breaks the
moons into 72 fragments (ARI 0.01) and HDBSCAN drops the sparse tips as noise (ARI 0.71), because the

\paragraph{K-Means++ and GMM on D4: compact classes in seven dimensions.} At the number of classes, GMM
(ARI 0.618) and K-Means++ (0.508) match the localisation sites best of the six methods; with $K=7$,
K-Means++ reaches 0.731, the best result on D4. At that setting it keeps 96\% of the largest class
(cytoplasm, 143 proteins) in one cluster, 86 to 91\% of the three mid-sized classes, and all 5 proteins of
the outer-membrane lipoproteins together. They succeed because the classes are compact groups in seven
dimensions that touch, with no density gap between them: the case centroid methods are built for, and the
case where a density threshold fails (Section~\ref{sec:dims}). The inner-membrane proteins (77) are the weak spot:
they overlap the related inner-membrane classes, and only 52\% end up in one cluster.
% Sources: results/hdb_resale/ablation_features.csv, ablation_flat_design_clusters.csv
% (variant "floor area and lease only").
\paragraph{HDBSCAN on D7, floor area and lease only: flat designs.} This feature set was chosen after
seeing the main results, so we present it as an ablation finding and a sanity check, not as a tuned
setting. Dropped to two features, D7 does contain dense spots: standard flat designs, built to fixed
plans in particular years. HDBSCAN finds 40 clusters (25.3\% noise), and 37 of them are at least 97\% one
flat type, 27 of them entirely so (Figure~\ref{fig:flat_designs}); in the median cluster one flat model
accounts for 82\% of sales. A simple grouping by flat model and lease would give similar groups, which is
the point: the method recovers a structure we can name. ARI against flat type is still only 0.081,
because each type is split into several designs and the noise counts as a cluster. A low ARI here
reflects a finer partition than the labels, not a failure. On shuffled columns the same setting finds
38 clusters that match nothing (ARI 0.001).

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{hdb_success_flat_designs}
\caption{D7, success of HDBSCAN on floor area and remaining lease only (\texttt{min\_cluster\_size}~100,
\texttt{min\_samples}~10; grey: noise). Each cluster is one standard flat design; the legend gives its main
flat type and model. Cause of success: fixed building plans create real dense spots in these two features.}

\end{figure}

\subsubsection{Failures}
\paragraph{K-Means++ on D2: unrelated belts merged.} Shown in Figure~\ref{fig:kmeans_belts}. One
cluster joins five belts whose centres are 2{,}932 to 11{,}449\,km apart, from Iceland to the
Mid-Indian Ridge. Cause: cluster shape. Compact regions cannot follow long belts.
\paragraph{DBSCAN on D1: the silhouette's choice.} Shown in Figure~\ref{fig:lam-d1}. Among the settings
within the noise cap, the silhouette is highest for a radius so small that the moons break into 72
fragments, with 47\% of points called noise (ARI 0.009). A wider radius recovers both moons exactly, with a
lower silhouette. Cause: the silhouette rewards small, tight clusters and ignores the noise, so the rule
prefers fragments to the true curved shapes.
\paragraph{Density methods on D7: one mass, or storey bands.} Shown in Figure~\ref{fig:hdb_cmp}. At the chosen settings DBSCAN puts 96\% of sales in one cluster. On the recorded
values HDBSCAN returns 37 clusters, 34 of them single storey bands. Causes: no gaps between groups, and
values recorded in steps.
\paragraph{DBSCAN on D5.} % results/datasets/fashion_mnist_results.csv
DBSCAN puts three quarters of the images, from nearly every class, into one cluster and 23\% into noise
(ARI 0.028; Figure~\ref{fig:lam-d5}). The cause is one global $\varepsilon$ in 50 dimensions: an
$\varepsilon$ large enough to connect points within a class also connects the overlapping classes to each
other, and a smaller one turns most points into noise. The $k$-based methods, which are not tied to a
density threshold, reach ARI 0.34--0.40 on the same data.

\paragraph{HDBSCAN on D4: rare classes called noise.} Even at its best setting by ARI, a minimum
cluster size of 12, HDBSCAN returns two clusters and calls 37\% of proteins noise (ARI 0.339),
with a silhouette (0.483) higher than that of any method that assigns every protein. The noise is not
spread evenly. It takes 95\% of the outer-membrane class (20 proteins), every protein of two of the three
smallest classes (5 and 2), and about half of the inner-membrane and periplasm classes. Cause: a class of
2 to 20 proteins in seven dimensions is not dense enough to form a cluster of 12, so HDBSCAN discards
exactly the classes that are hardest to find, and the silhouette, which skips noise, rewards it. The
setting the rule picks without labels avoids the noise only by putting 97\% of proteins in one cluster
(ARI 0.038).

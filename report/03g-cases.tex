\subsection{Success and failure cases}\label{sec:cases}

% Rubric: "illustrate and discuss the successful cases and failure cases", "good visualizations".
% Every number comes from results/datasets/<dataset>_results.csv or <dataset>_tuning.csv.

\subsubsection{Successes}
\paragraph{Spectral on D1: curved clusters.} Shown in Figure~\ref{fig:d1}. Spectral clustering on a
10-nearest-neighbour graph recovers both moons exactly (ARI 1.000). It succeeds because each moon is a
chain of close neighbours, and the gap between the moons is wider than any link in the chain. K-Means++
and GMM fail on the same data (ARI 0.481 and 0.501) because they assume convex clusters.
\paragraph{HDBSCAN on D2: belts of varying density.} Shown in Figure~\ref{fig:d2}. HDBSCAN returns 28
clusters that follow the plate boundaries, none holding more than 7.5\% of earthquakes, with a silhouette
of 0.756 against 0.317 on fake data. It succeeds because its density threshold adapts to each cluster,
the property D2 needs most; DBSCAN, with one radius, joins the dense western Pacific into one cluster of
35.7\%.
\paragraph{GMM on D4 and D6: compact classes that touch.} GMM has the best ARI on both (0.618 on D4,
0.915 on D6), ahead of K-Means++ (0.508 and 0.897). The classes are compact groups with no density gap
between them, the case centroid and mixture models are built for, and GMM's per-component covariance fits
classes of different spread and orientation better than K-means' round clusters.

\subsubsection{Failures}
\paragraph{DBSCAN on D1: the silhouette picks fragments.} Shown in Figure~\ref{fig:d1}. The selection
rule picks $\varepsilon=0.049$, which breaks the moons into 72 fragments with 47.1\% noise (ARI 0.009),
although the same grid contains a setting with ARI 1.000. Cause: the silhouette scores 72 tight fragments
(0.523) above two curved moons (0.387). The fake-data check flags it: shuffled data score 0.822 at the
same setting.
\paragraph{Density methods on D3 and D8: one mass.} Shown in Figure~\ref{fig:d8}. With no gaps between
groups, DBSCAN puts 97.7\% of pickups (D3) and 99.7\% of sales (D8) in one cluster, and HDBSCAN 91.5\% and
99.7\%. Cause: no gaps between groups. On D8 the silhouette (0.438, 0.496) and the fake-data check both
approve the result; only the largest-cluster share reveals it.
\paragraph{Density methods on D5: high dimensions.} DBSCAN and HDBSCAN each return two clusters with 23\%
to 33\% noise and ARI 0.028 and 0.023, while the $k$-based methods reach 0.34 to 0.40
(Figure~\ref{fig:d5}). Cause: in 50 dimensions an $\varepsilon$ large enough to connect points within a
class also connects the overlapping classes, and no setting in the grid does better than ARI 0.066.

\paragraph{HDBSCAN on D4: rare classes lost.} At the setting chosen without labels HDBSCAN puts 97.0\%
of proteins in one cluster (ARI 0.038) and still has the highest silhouette on D4 (0.559). Even its best
setting by ARI (\texttt{min\_samples} 10) reaches only 0.398 by calling 29.8\% of proteins noise. Cause: a
class of 2 to 20 proteins in seven dimensions is not dense enough to form a cluster of its own, so
HDBSCAN discards exactly the classes that are hardest to find, and the silhouette, which skips noise,
rewards it.

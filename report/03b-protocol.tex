\paragraph{Metrics.}
% [Wei Yew]: the columns below are the shared schema for every table in Section 3.3. Calinski-Harabasz
% is left out because not every member computed it.
Every result table reports the same columns. \emph{Internal} scores need no labels. The silhouette
coefficient~\cite{rousseeuw1987} of a point is $(b-a)/\max(a,b)$, where $a$ is its mean distance to its
own cluster and $b$ its mean distance to the nearest other cluster; we report the mean over points, from
$-1$ to $1$, higher is better. The Davies--Bouldin index~\cite{davies1979} averages, over clusters, the
worst ratio of within-cluster spread to between-centre distance; lower is better. Both are computed on
clustered points only, so they say nothing about the points a density method calls noise. Two
\emph{coverage} figures therefore stand next to them: the noise fraction and the share of points in the
largest cluster. \emph{External} scores need labels. The Adjusted Rand Index (ARI)~\cite{hubert1985} counts
pairs of points on which a clustering and the labels agree, corrected so that a random clustering scores
about 0 and a perfect one 1. Normalised mutual information (NMI) measures the same agreement in terms of
shared information. For both, noise counts as one more cluster, so a method cannot raise its score by
discarding hard points. We also report the fit time.

\paragraph{Fake-data check.}
An internal score has no fixed scale: a silhouette of 0.3 can be strong on one dataset and meaningless on
another. Each chosen setting is therefore run again on fake data that keeps each feature's distribution but
has no joint structure. For tables, each column is shuffled independently. For coordinates, points are
drawn uniformly in the same latitude and longitude range. A result counts as structure only if the real data
score clearly above the fake data.

\paragraph{Selection rule.}
% Sources: weiyew/sc4020-earthquake/results/dbscan_grid.csv (min_samples 10, eps quantile 0.25);
% weiyew/sc4020-hdb/results/hdbscan_grid.csv (min_cluster_size 400, min_samples None, real).
One rule picks the setting of DBSCAN and HDBSCAN on every dataset: among the settings that give at least
2 clusters and label at most 50\% of points as noise, take the one with the highest silhouette. The noise
cap is needed because the silhouette ignores noise. Without it, the silhouette prefers settings that throw
most of the data away. On D2 it would pick DBSCAN with $\varepsilon=20.4$\,km, which scores 0.878 by
calling 69.4\% of earthquakes noise. On D8 it would pick HDBSCAN with \texttt{min\_cluster\_size}~400,
which scores 0.448 with 70.9\% of sales as noise. For K-means, $K$ is chosen from the elbow in inertia,
the silhouette, the gap to the fake-data silhouette and agreement between seeds; each dataset states which
of these decided. On labelled data we report the rule's choice and the setting with the best ARI, so the
cost of choosing without labels is visible.

\paragraph{Parameter grids.}
% Sources: weiyew/sc4020-earthquake/results/{kmeans_k_selection,dbscan_grid,hdbscan_grid}.csv;
% weiyew/sc4020-hdb/results/{kmeans_k_sweep,dbscan_grid,hdbscan_grid}.csv.
K-means and K-means++: $K=2$ to 12, 10 starts each. DBSCAN: \texttt{minPts} (\texttt{min\_samples} in
scikit-learn) in $\{5, 10, 20\}$. Its radius $\varepsilon$ comes from the k-distance curve, the sorted
distance from each point to its \texttt{minPts}-th neighbour: on D2 we take the 25th, 50th, 75th, 90th and
95th percentiles of the curve (11 to 810\,km); on D8 we take 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5 and 2 times the knee of the
curve. HDBSCAN: \texttt{min\_cluster\_size} scaled to the data (25, 50, 100 on D2; 100, 200, 400 on D8)
and \texttt{min\_samples} in \{default, 5, 10\}.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
On D2 and D8 every random step uses seed 0, and repeated runs use seeds 0 to 19. They ran on Python
3.12.13, scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011} 1.9.1 and NumPy 2.5.3 on an Apple M2 with 16\,GB of memory.
On D3 and D4 the same library versions ran on Python 3.12.10, on an Intel Core Ultra 5 225 with 32\,GB
of memory; every random step uses seed 42, and repeated runs use seeds 0 to 29.

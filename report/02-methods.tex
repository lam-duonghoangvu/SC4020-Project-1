\subsection{K-Means and K-Means++}
% Ky. Code: src/sc4020/methods.py (kmeans, kmeans_random_init), src/sc4020/tuning.py (select_kmeans_k_by_null_gap)
\paragraph{How it works.} K-means~\cite{lloyd1982} finds $k$ centres that minimise the within-cluster sum
of squares $J=\sum_{i}\min_j\lVert\mathbf{x}_i-\boldsymbol{\mu}_j\rVert^2$: assign each point to its
nearest centre, move each centre to the mean of its points, repeat. $J$ never rises, so it stops at a
local optimum that depends on the start. Random starts pick $k$ points uniformly; K-means++~\cite{arthur2007}
picks each next centre with probability proportional to its squared distance from the nearest chosen one.
One iteration costs $O(nkd)$.
\paragraph{Pros.} Fast and simple; scales to large $n$; K-means++ makes a good optimum more likely.
\paragraph{Cons.} Assumes compact, round clusters of similar spread: cuts curved or elongated ones. Needs
$k$, assigns every point (no noise), and depends on feature scale.
\paragraph{Our implementation.} \texttt{kmeans} (K-means++) and \texttt{kmeans\_random\_init} wrap
scikit-learn's \texttt{KMeans} with \texttt{n\_init}=10 and seed 42. Parameter assessed: $k\in\{2,\dots,10\}$
(a wider grid up to 50 on D2), chosen by \texttt{select\_kmeans\_k\_by\_null\_gap}: among $k$ stable across
10 seeds (pairwise ARI $\geq0.95$), the largest silhouette gap over column-shuffled data. On labelled
data $k$ is the number of classes.

\subsection{DBSCAN}
% Ky. Code: src/sc4020/methods.py (dbscan), src/sc4020/tuning.py (select_dbscan_by_shared_rule, k_distance_curve, find_knee)
\paragraph{How it works.} DBSCAN~\cite{ester1996} takes a radius $\varepsilon$ and a count \texttt{minPts}.
A point with at least \texttt{minPts} neighbours within $\varepsilon$ is a core point; core points within
$\varepsilon$ of each other form a cluster, points near a core point join it as border points, and the
rest are noise. With a spatial index a fit costs about $O(n\log n)$ in few dimensions, up to $O(n^2)$.
\paragraph{Pros.} No $k$; any cluster shape; labels noise; on coordinates $\varepsilon$ is a distance in km.
\paragraph{Cons.} One radius for all clusters: sparse clusters become noise or dense ones merge. Needs
gaps between clusters, and weakens in many dimensions, where distances become alike~\cite{beyer1999}.
\paragraph{Our implementation.} \texttt{dbscan} wraps scikit-learn's \texttt{DBSCAN} (Euclidean, or
haversine with a ball tree on coordinates in radians). Parameters assessed: \texttt{min\_samples}
around $\mathrm{clip}(2d,4,20)$ (half, once, twice and four times it) and $\varepsilon$ at
0.5 to 4 times the knee of the k-distance curve (\texttt{k\_distance\_curve}, \texttt{find\_knee}),
chosen by \texttt{select\_dbscan\_by\_shared\_rule}: among settings with at least 2 clusters and at most
50\% noise, the highest silhouette.

\subsection{HDBSCAN}
% Ky. Code: src/sc4020/methods.py (hdbscan), src/sc4020/tuning.py (select_hdbscan_by_shared_rule)
\paragraph{How it works.} HDBSCAN~\cite{campello2013} removes $\varepsilon$. With the core distance of
a point (to its $m$-th neighbour), the mutual reachability distance
$d_{\mathrm{mr}}(a,b)=\max\{\mathrm{core}(a),\mathrm{core}(b),d(a,b)\}$ pushes sparse points apart. Its
minimum spanning tree holds DBSCAN's clusterings at every density; splits smaller than
\texttt{min\_cluster\_size} are dropped and the clusters that persist longest are kept, the rest is
noise. A fit costs between $O(n\log n)$ and $O(n^2)$.
\paragraph{Pros.} No $k$ or $\varepsilon$; clusters of different density in one run; labels noise.
\paragraph{Cons.} \texttt{min\_cluster\_size} is a count, so results change with sample size; can call
large parts of the data noise; finds nothing where there are no density gaps.
\paragraph{Our implementation.} \texttt{hdbscan} wraps scikit-learn's \texttt{HDBSCAN}. Parameters
assessed: \texttt{min\_cluster\_size} $\in\{5,10,15,20,30,50,100,200,400\}$ up to $n/10$, and
\texttt{min\_samples} $\in\{$default, 5, 10$\}$, chosen by \texttt{select\_hdbscan\_by\_shared\_rule}.

\subsection{Gaussian Mixture Model}
% Lam. Code: src/sc4020/methods.py (gmm), src/sc4020/tuning.py (select_gmm_by_bic)
\paragraph{How it works.} A Gaussian mixture model (GMM) models the data as
$p(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi_j\,\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}\mid\boldsymbol{\mu}_j,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_j)$,
fitted by expectation--maximisation~\cite{dempster1977}: the E-step computes each point's probability
under every component, the M-step re-estimates weights, means and covariances. Each point goes to its most
probable component. One iteration with full covariances costs $O(nkd^2+kd^3)$.
\paragraph{Pros.} Ellipsoidal clusters of different size, orientation and weight; soft assignments; BIC
gives a principled way to choose $k$.
\paragraph{Cons.} Each cluster must be one convex blob: fails on curved or nested shapes. Needs $k$, has
no noise label, and full covariances need many points per component when $d$ is large.
\paragraph{Our implementation.} \texttt{gmm} wraps scikit-learn's \texttt{GaussianMixture}~\cite{pedregosa2011}
with \texttt{n\_init}=3 and seed 42. Parameters assessed: $k$ over the same grid as K-means and
\texttt{covariance\_type} $\in\{$full, tied, diag, spherical$\}$, chosen by lowest BIC
(\texttt{select\_gmm\_by\_bic}); on labelled data $k$ is the number of classes.

\subsection{Spectral clustering}
% Lam. Code: src/sc4020/methods.py (spectral); affinity per dataset in src/sc4020/run.py (SPECTRAL_AFFINITY_BY_DATASET)
\paragraph{How it works.} Spectral clustering~\cite{ng2002} builds an affinity matrix between points,
takes the $k$ leading eigenvectors of its normalised graph Laplacian as a new embedding, and runs K-means
there. Points linked by chains of close neighbours land together, even if far apart in the input. A dense
affinity costs $O(n^2)$ memory and up to $O(n^3)$ time; a sparse neighbour graph is cheaper.
\paragraph{Pros.} Recovers curved and non-convex clusters; a neighbour graph adapts to local scale.
\paragraph{Cons.} Needs $k$; no noise label; sensitive to the affinity; quadratic cost limits $n$.
\paragraph{Our implementation.} \texttt{spectral} wraps scikit-learn's \texttt{SpectralClustering}
(seed 42). Affinity fixed per dataset: RBF with $\gamma=1$ on D6, a 10-nearest-neighbour graph elsewhere.
$k$ is taken from the K-means selection, not tuned. Skipped above 10{,}000 points.

\subsection{Agglomerative clustering}
\habegin
% Source: experiments/01_scikit_learn_wine_clustering.ipynb; ARI from results/datasets/wine_results.csv.
% Code: src/sc4020/methods.py (agglomerative)
\paragraph{How it works.} \hatag{}Every point starts as its own cluster and the two closest clusters are
merged until $k$ remain. With Ward linkage~\cite{ward1963} the merged pair is the one that raises the
within-cluster variance least: the K-means objective, searched greedily bottom-up. It needs $O(n^2)$ time
and memory.
\paragraph{Pros.} No starting centres, so the same partition on every run: a direct check on K-means.
\paragraph{Cons.} Assumes compact clusters, no noise label, and a merge is never undone; quadratic cost.
On D6 it matched the labels less well than K-means (ARI 0.790 against 0.897).
\paragraph{Our implementation.} \texttt{agglomerative} wraps scikit-learn's \texttt{AgglomerativeClustering}
with Ward linkage on Euclidean distances. $k$ is taken from the K-means selection, not tuned. Skipped
above 10{,}000 points.

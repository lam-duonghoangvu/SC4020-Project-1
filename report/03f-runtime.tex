\subsection{Runtime and scalability}\label{sec:runtime}

% Sources: results/hdb_resale/scalability_summary.csv (median of runs per size, fixed settings);
% neighbour-pair estimates from experiments/12_data_gov_hdb_resale_ablation.ipynb.
D7 is large enough to measure how each method grows. K-means, DBSCAN and HDBSCAN ran at fixed settings on
random subsets of 1{,}000 to 234{,}292 sales (K-means from one start). Figure~\ref{fig:runtime} plots the
median time against size on log scales, where the slope of a line estimates the exponent $b$ in
$t \propto n^{b}$ (fitted from 5{,}000 sales up). K-means is far cheaper than either density method at
every size: under 0.08\,s on all 234{,}292 sales. Its fitted slopes (0.85 and 0.64) are below 1 only
because a fixed start-up cost dominates at these sizes; each iteration is linear in $n$. HDBSCAN grows
with slope 2.04, from 0.007\,s at 1{,}000 sales to 222\,s at 234{,}292. DBSCAN grows with slope 1.71, but
above 50{,}000 sales it was not run at all, because of memory rather than time. With a fixed
$\varepsilon$, every sale has more neighbours as the data grow: estimated from probe points, 100{,}000 sales
would hold about 178 million neighbour pairs and the full data about 943 million, some 7.5\,GB for the
neighbour indices alone, which scikit-learn's DBSCAN stores in full. HDBSCAN, slower at every size, did run
on all sales.

% Source: results/datasets/fashion_mnist_results.csv (fit_seconds; one fit at the chosen setting).
D5 was sampled down from 70{,}000 to 3{,}000 images because three methods scale badly with $n$ in
50 dimensions. Spectral clustering needs a graph over all points and an eigendecomposition that grows
faster than linearly, and a dense affinity matrix at $n=70{,}000$ would take about 39\,GB in double
precision. DBSCAN and HDBSCAN rely on neighbour queries, and in 50 dimensions spatial indexes prune
little, so these queries approach the $O(n^2)$ brute-force cost. On the 3{,}000-point sample one fit takes
0.02\,s for DBSCAN, 0.34\,s for K-Means++, 0.38\,s for HDBSCAN, 1.4\,s for spectral clustering and 34\,s
for GMM. GMM is by far the slowest here: with full covariances each EM step costs $O(nkd^2)$ with $d=50$,
and it runs three starts. These times exclude parameter search, which refits each method once per
setting tried.

% Sources: results/hotspot/shared_rule.csv; results/comparison/gmm_spectral.csv (fit_seconds).
On the other datasets one fit takes at most a few seconds. On D3 (15{,}000 points) it takes 0.03 to
0.04\,s for K-means, 0.12\,s for GMM, 0.19\,s for spectral clustering, 0.48\,s for DBSCAN and 1.3\,s for
HDBSCAN; on D7 (20{,}000 sales) GMM takes 1.2\,s and spectral clustering 0.5\,s; on D4 every method takes
under 0.05\,s (Tables~\ref{tab:cmp_d2d7}--\ref{tab:cmp_labelled}). At these sizes runtime does not decide
the choice of method; at the size of the full D7, it does.

% ms per 1,000 points = fit seconds / n x 10^6.
Runtime is compared only where a method reaches ARI $>0.7$, which happens on D1 and D6 only; it is one
fit's time per 1{,}000 points at the rule's setting (Table~\ref{tab:runtime}).
In the case where ARI $>0.9$, only one method qualifies on each dataset: Spectral on D1 (14\,ms) and GMM
on D6 (39\,ms). In the case where ARI $>0.7$, the ranking from fastest is Spectral (14\,ms), then HDBSCAN
(18\,ms) on D1, and K-Means (26\,ms), then K-Means++ (33\,ms), then GMM (39\,ms) on D6.

On small data a fixed start-up cost dominates, so these times say little about scaling. The largest
costs come from model size, not from $n$: GMM with full covariances in 50 dimensions takes 14.7\,s on the
3{,}000 images of D5, against at most 0.33\,s for every other method there. On the 20{,}000 sales of D8
one fit takes 2.4\,s for Spectral on a nearest-neighbour graph, 1.6\,s for HDBSCAN, 1.5\,s for GMM and
0.6\,s for DBSCAN. Choosing the setting costs far more than fitting it: selecting $K$ for K-means, which
fits every $K$ with 10 seeds and on 3 fake copies, takes 126\,s on D3 and 130\,s on D8. A dense RBF
affinity for Spectral would need memory quadratic in $n$, so it is used only on D6; the
nearest-neighbour graph is sparse and ran on every dataset.

\subsection{Key factors and practical guidance}\label{sec:factors}

% Rubric: "discuss the key factors that affect the performance".

% [Ky]: ranked by how much each factor changed the outcome; every number is quoted from the section cited.
\paragraph{Key factors.} Ordered by the size of their effect in our experiments:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Cluster shape against the method's assumption.} On D1 K-Means++ reaches ARI 0.481 and GMM
0.501, against 1.000 for Spectral; on D2 K-means and Spectral draw regions that join unrelated belts
(Sections~\ref{sec:shape} and \ref{sec:density}). No choice of $K$ repairs a wrong assumption about shape.
\item \textbf{Gaps between groups.} Density methods need empty space between clusters. Where there is
none they return one dominant cluster: 99.7\% of sales on D8, 97.7\% of pickups on D3 and 97.0\% of
proteins on D4 (Sections~\ref{sec:nogaps}, \ref{sec:city} and \ref{sec:rare}).
\item \textbf{Dimensionality.} On D5, with 50 dimensions, both density methods fall to ARI 0.03 or below,
and no setting in their grids exceeds 0.066; the $k$-based methods reach 0.34 to 0.40
(Section~\ref{sec:dims}).
\item \textbf{Parameter choice without labels.} The rule's choice can be far from the best setting in the
same grid: DBSCAN on D1 scores ARI 0.009 against 1.000, HDBSCAN on D4 0.038 against 0.398, and GMM on D4
0.618 at the number of classes against 0.741 at 7 components (Sections~\ref{sec:shape} and \ref{sec:rare}).
\item \textbf{Uneven density.} On D2 HDBSCAN follows the belts with 28 clusters, none above 7.5\% of
earthquakes, while one DBSCAN radius joins the dense western Pacific into a cluster of 35.7\%
(Section~\ref{sec:density}).
\item \textbf{Initialisation.} At the same $K$ and with 10 starts each, K-Means++ matches the labels better
than random starts on D4 (ARI 0.508 against 0.379) and is within 0.001 of it on D1, D5 and D6
(Table~\ref{tab:cmp_labelled}).
\item \textbf{Runtime.} It matters only for GMM with full covariances in many dimensions (14.7\,s on D5)
and for parameter search, which costs more than 100 times a single fit (Section~\ref{sec:runtime}).
\end{enumerate}
\paragraph{Practical guidance.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Choose by the structure of the data, not by a score.} Plot the data or a projection first.
If the groups are curved or long, no choice of $k$ will help K-means; if they are compact and touching,
or the data have more than a handful of dimensions, no choice of $\varepsilon$ will help DBSCAN.
\item \textbf{Do not pick settings by the silhouette alone.} It favours tight fragments (D1 DBSCAN) and
settings that call hard points noise. First restrict the search to what the analysis needs (a noise cap,
a minimum number of clusters, a cap on the largest cluster), then compare within that range.
\item \textbf{Report the noise fraction and the largest cluster next to every silhouette.} A silhouette
computed after discarding 42\% of the points (D6 HDBSCAN), or with 99.7\% in one cluster (D8), says little.
\item \textbf{Compare every result against fake data.} Rerun the chosen setting on shuffled columns. It
catches results with no real structure (D1 DBSCAN, D4 and D5 HDBSCAN), though not one dominant cluster
(D8) or the wrong structure (D2 K-means).
\item \textbf{Standardise before K-means, and use K-means++ with several starts.} Random starts lose ARI on
D4 even with 10 starts.
\item \textbf{Represent coordinates so that distance means something.} Unit vectors on the sphere for
global coordinates (D2), a local projection to kilometres within a city (D3); a DBSCAN radius in
kilometres can then be checked by someone who knows the area.
\end{enumerate}

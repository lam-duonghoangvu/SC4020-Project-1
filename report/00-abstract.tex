\begin{abstract}
\noindent
% [Ky]: draft (about 230 words) following the thesis proposed below; revise once the title is decided.
Clustering methods differ in what they take a cluster to be, and real data break those ideas in
different ways. We compare K-means, K-means++, DBSCAN, HDBSCAN, Gaussian mixtures and spectral clustering
on seven datasets, each chosen to test one data property: curved shapes, long belts of uneven density, a
dense city with no gaps, rare classes, high dimensions, and data with no gaps between groups. Every
method is tuned by one label-free rule. We ask where each method fails, whether the scores available
without labels reveal those failures, and whether a method built for the property repairs them. Each
method failed where the data broke its idea of a cluster. K-means cut the curved classes of make\_moons
(ARI 0.48 against 1.00 for spectral clustering); one DBSCAN radius chained the dense western Pacific into
one cluster of 36\% of earthquakes while HDBSCAN followed the plate belts; and in 50 dimensions the
density methods fell to ARI 0.03 or below. The silhouette did not reveal these failures: on all four
labelled datasets the method with the top silhouette was not the one that matched the labels best, and on
make\_moons it chose a DBSCAN setting that breaks the moons into 72 fragments (ARI 0.01). Rerunning each
result on shuffled data caught methods that found nothing real, but not one dominant cluster or the wrong
structure. No method produced meaningful clusters in data with no gaps. The choice of method should
follow from the structure of the data, and every score needs a check against fake data and against the
share of the largest cluster.

\end{abstract}
